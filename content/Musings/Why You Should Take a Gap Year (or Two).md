# Introduction

The hope behind writing this is that the reader walks away less hesitant to take a year (or some other amount of time) off of work, if this is already something that they were considering.

In my experience talking to my friends and peers, it seems that people are usually very hesitant to take a gap year and 'lose time', even when there are compelling reasons for them to take some time off. I would like to make the case that:
- Taking a gap year, by choice or by necessity, is not the end of the world
- Gap years can sometimes be the optimal choice for one's long term growth
- Everybody learns, works, and lives differently, and what is important is for each individual to figure out the environments in which _they_ thrive; there is no one-size-fits-all here.

Up to this point in my academic career, I have taken effectively two gap years. One was before beginning my undergraduate degree, and I also took one semester off right in the middle of the degree. As a result, I'll be graduating in a fall semester instead of a spring semester, so I have one more gap semester coming up half a year from now.

I believe that, both times, both in the moment and in hindsight, choosing to take the gap year was optimal, and that I have ended up in a much better place, mathematically and mental health-wise, than if I had not taken the gap years. I'll explain my reasoning in more detail and why I claim that the choices were good in a bit, but the general principle behind the decisions was the following:

>[!important] Principle
>Short term strength snowballs into long term strength.

After this, I'll write about taking gap years in general, and how I think they should be thought of and used in order to grow from them.
